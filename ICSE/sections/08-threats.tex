\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Several threats temper our claims. The principal one is \emph{external}: our
telemetry-rich datasets are synthetic while our real dataset is text only, so
on-demand evidence-gathering is exercised only on injected faults and we
do not claim it transfers to production telemetry; the open-source Apache
incidents and two demonstration applications are not a representative sample of
all deployments.

On \emph{construct} validity there are two distinct threats, and we separate
them. First, the real dataset has no ground-truth answer key, so matches are
inferred from project, component, and symptom overlap. We report both relations,
show that the rule does not leak into the systems compared
(\S\ref{sec:results-leakage}), and give random-ranker reference points, but the
relation measures topical relatedness rather than diagnostic usefulness, and our
LLM-as-judge probe is explicitly not a substitute for human judgement: its
controls are easy, and it rates only $6.3\%$ of coarse-gold pairs highly. No
human has annotated relevance in this work. We release the sampling script,
rubric, and annotation schema so that a multi-annotator study can be run, and
report that study as necessary future work rather than claiming its conclusion.

Second, and separately, our real triage labels are derived from a ticket's final
Jira resolution, which is not the same construct as on-call page-worthiness. An
issue closed \textsc{Won't Fix} may still have demanded operational
investigation, and a fixed bug may never have warranted a page. Our real-data
triage results should therefore be read as predicting \emph{eventual resolution
status from ticket text}, and we do not generalize them to whether an alert
should have paged an engineer; establishing that would require independently
annotated operational urgency, which this corpus does not carry.

For \emph{internal}
validity, time-aware visibility and held-out fault families and project domains
prevent leakage of future or training information, though only the costly
real-data retriever is trained on a single seed (the two telemetry retrievers are
stable across three seeds, \S\ref{sec:results-rq1}), which we report.
Finally, for \emph{conclusion} validity, every difference is tested with a paired
bootstrap corrected for false discovery, and we report negative and mixed results,
such as fusion's lack of benefit on small corpora, the absence of any measurable
benefit from the evidence-gathering tools, and triage's weakness on real
data, rather than reporting only favorable comparisons.
