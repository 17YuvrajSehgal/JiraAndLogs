We thank the reviewers for their careful feedback and for recognizing the problem's relevance and the value of budgeted reasoning. The reviews point to a clear path: directly evaluate the practical usefulness of retrieved incidents, sharpen the interpretation of real-data results, and expand methodological and reproducibility details. We request a major revision to complete the work below.
Reviewers A/B: Relevance labels and practical usefulness
The 0.970 Hit@5 on World of Logs (5,150 queries, 38,642 memory tickets) measures retrieval under an inferred relevance rule: same project and at least one shared Jira component. We will label it as such throughout, including the abstract, and distinguish it from diagnostic or resolution usefulness.
On overlap between labels and graph retrieval: the graph uses LLM-extracted entities from ticket text, not the Jira component field. Graph-only retrieval achieves 0.308 Hit@5, and removing it from fusion causes no significant change. Under a stricter rule adding symptom overlap, hybrid retrieval achieves 0.74 Hit@5 (Kafka 0.80, MariaDB 0.73). We will report all baselines under both definitions and quantify missing component metadata and its effect on eligibility and results.
Our top priority is a blinded human evaluation of top-ranked results from ARISE and the strongest baselines. Raters will judge whether retrieved incidents provide useful diagnostic or resolution information, using hard same-project alternatives, a documented rubric, and inter-rater agreement. The existing Qwen2.5-7B assessment (300 labeled and 300 random pairs per dataset; about 2.6/5 mean for labeled pairs) will be presented as exploratory.
Reviewers A/B: Real-data classification and paging
The telemetry-based classifiers expect numerical features that are absent from World of Logs. In the evaluated pipeline, these inputs were zero-filled, producing constant predictions. We will remove the claim that their chance-level performance shows that classification fails on real incidents and replace this invalid comparison with text-based classifiers trained on ticket descriptions under the same held-out-project partition.
Specifically, in the abstract and introduction, we will replace statements that ticket-worthiness classification “does not transfer” with a narrower description of the text-based, resolution-label prediction task and its measured results. In RQ4 and the discussion, we will replace the synthetic-versus-real classifier comparison with the text-based baseline results and state that the original zero-filled comparison cannot support a transfer conclusion.
ARISE’s reported 0.506 triage accuracy is a separate result based on retrieval-derived scores on text-only inputs; the zero-filled-feature explanation applies only to the telemetry-based classifiers. We will clarify this distinction in the experimental setup and RQ4 results.
Jira resolution status and operational page-worthiness are different concepts. We will rename the real-data target “resolution-derived labels” in the dataset description, results, and conclusion, and remove claims that these labels measure real-world paging effectiveness. Assessing page-worthiness would require independently annotated operational urgency, which this corpus does not provide.
Reviewer B: MAXCONF and thresholds
MAXCONF(R) is the maximum of available retrievers' triage_score values (default zero), separate from the fused RRF score. The 0.5 gate therefore does not apply to the raw RRF score, whose maximum the reviewer correctly computes as about 0.049. For the fine-tuned dense retriever, the triage score comes from a logistic head over similarity features; hybrid retrieval also computes it separately. We will specify features, training, score range, and fallbacks for each configuration in Section IV-A and Algorithm 1.
The 0.90 and 0.5 thresholds were fixed in advance, not tuned on test results. We will add sensitivity analyses for retrieval quality, escalation frequency, and cost, and align the algorithm, implementation, and evaluated controller behavior.
Reviewer B: Cost estimates
The 40–78% reductions are estimates from a per-skill cost model applied to controller decisions. We will correct the "measured" wording in the Table III caption and Section VI-B. We will report per-step invocation and skip counts with denominators and cost assumptions and add measured wall-clock time and token usage for adaptive and run-all configurations, with offline training costs reported separately.
Reviewer C: Repeated runs and training
Section VII-A reports three-seed results for the telemetry datasets (0.632 ± 0.015 and 0.678 ± 0.004). We will extend World of Logs to multiple seeds. Retriever fine-tuning is offline, and the model is frozen during evaluation. We will make this explicit, add confidence intervals to Figure 4 separating query-level from training-run variation, and document fixed seeds and greedy decoding.
Reviewer C: Incident construction, labels, and presentation
Each memory item is a resolved ticket: a historical Jira issue (World of Logs) or a ticket generated for an injected fault (telemetry datasets). Synthetic labels come from fault injections; Jira classification labels from issue type and resolution; retrieval relevance from the project/component rule and its stricter variant. The LLM judge evaluates sampled pairs only and does not produce benchmark labels. No human relevance judgments are currently used. We will correct the annotation-kit statement in Section IX and release the human-study protocol and materials.
We will provide the full judge prompt and sampling procedure, use one name for Hybrid-RRF, rewrite the flagged introduction passages, and clarify how continuous streams are grouped into decision windows and how timestamped memory is available to each decision.
Reviewer A: Related systems
We will compare FLASH, Triangle, DeepCT, and COMET by objectives, inputs, outputs, and evaluation settings, positioning ARISE's contribution: combining historical incident retrieval with capability-aware, budgeted evidence gathering. Where tasks and inputs permit, we will add experimental comparisons and document any adaptations.
Reviewer A: Artifacts
v1–v3 were internal names for progressively larger corpus variants. Experiments use v3, while v2 is currently bundled. We will publish the exact v3 corpus on Zenodo with a matching version identifier, plus the manifest, query/memory partitions, preprocessing instructions, and configurations.
We believe these revisions directly address the reviewers' concerns about practical usefulness, comparison fairness, controller behavior, and reproducibility, and respectfully request a major revision.
