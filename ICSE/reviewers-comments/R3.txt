Paper summary
The authors promote a reasoning and retrieval system that helps links current incidents to past incidents. It includes a budgetter that helps budget how much reasoning and work should be done. The authors demonstrate on 3 datasets the effectiveness of the approach.

Strengths

Multiple Runs
The authors claims:

Implementation. The dense retriever is a compact MiniLM bi- encoder fine-tuned for five epochs with hard negatives mined by lexical search; the hybrid fuses each retriever’s top candidates by reciprocal rank fusion. Graph entities are extracted from tickets by a small language model and matched by entity overlap. The retrieval-augmented and judge models run offline with deterministic (greedy) decoding. Every experiment uses a fixed random seed, and full configurations and seeds are released with the artifact.

Which implies that perhaps we should see multiple runs and fixed seed is not really enough. If others deploy your algorithm they are going to have different seeds and choices. We're dealing with an intentionally stochastic process so we need to use statistics to evaluate performance.

Fine-tuning is used in the paper and it's not clear when that is done. It is said it done per incident? But is that learned network passed on to the next? How is the fine-tuned dense MiniLM actually dealt with? This seems fundamental non-deterministic, even with fixed seeds. So that implies we need to have to multiple runs of the same operation and produce statistics in order to compare performance. It isn't called stochastic gradient descent because it was deterministic. The authors use GPUs and multicore CPUs so there's no guarantee of determinism for many of these baselines and experiments.

Conclusions
There's lot of methodological issues and confounds. Parts of the paper will not be replicable by a research assistant due to lack of details. The evaluation would be stronger if it was repeated. Some statistics are helpful, such as the use of confidence intervals. There's a lack of clarity in the use of human labellers and when fine-tuning is applied.

The authors push a budgetting approach which is evaluated somewhat and seems to have some progress.
